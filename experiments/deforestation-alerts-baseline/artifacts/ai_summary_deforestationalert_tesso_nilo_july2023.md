# AI Alert Summary: Tesso Nilo National Park — July 2023

Below is an _LLM-generated deforestation alert summary_, the output of the
simple pipeline in this experiment. The prompt that produced this output is
provided in the file `prompt_template.md`. 

This specific output was shared with the Places to Watch team on January 21, 2024. 

Notes: 
* The model did not have access to the PTW reports
* The model did not have access to the Mongabay articles on Tesso Nilo NP.

The July 2023 alert data indicates a continuation of the elevated disturbance
pattern documented during the final months of the baseline period, and
represents a significant escalation compared to historical norms for Tesso Nilo
National Park.

July 2023 recorded 9,473 disturbance alert pixels, representing approximately
285 hectares of detected vegetation loss. This figure is 89% above the baseline
monthly average and nearly double the baseline median. More critically, it
represents an 88-fold increase compared to July 2020, demonstrating the
dramatic acceleration of disturbance activity within the park over the past
three years.

While July 2023 shows an 18% decline from the record-breaking June 2023 peak
of 11,508 pixels, this modest reduction should not be interpreted as a
de-escalation. Rather, July's activity remains well within the elevated range
that has characterized the park since mid-2021, and falls during the typical
peak disturbance season (April through September). The fact that alert levels
remain near 10,000 pixels immediately following the baseline maximum suggests
sustained, ongoing pressure rather than a temporary spike that has subsided.

The severity profile of July alerts warrants attention. Approximately 27% of
detected pixels fall into the highest severity category — the strongest
vegetation loss signals — while an additional 73% represent high-severity
alerts. Though the proportion of highest-severity pixels is slightly below the
baseline average of 31%, the absolute number of highest-severity pixels (2,516)
remains substantial and indicates significant vegetation removal rather than
marginal disturbance.

From a policy perspective, this data indicates a meaningful escalation that
differs from routine seasonal fluctuation in three important respects. First,
the magnitude substantially exceeds historical norms even accounting for
seasonal variation. Second, it represents a continuation rather than
interruption of the accelerating trend documented through June 2023. Third, it
occurs within a protected area already experiencing chronic encroachment
pressure, where the baseline itself reflects degradation rather than an intact
reference condition.

It is important to note that these are preliminary disturbance alerts, not
confirmed deforestation. The dataset is designed to flag anomalous vegetation
loss that may result from various causes, including agricultural clearing, fire,
or natural events. Classification data identifying the likely drivers of these
alerts will not be available for approximately 90 days. However, given Tesso
Nilo's documented history of illegal agricultural encroachment and its position
surrounded by oil palm plantations, there is reasonable cause for concern that
a significant fraction of these alerts represent unauthorized clearing.

This pattern warrants internal escalation and consideration of engagement with
government partners. The sustained high alert levels following the baseline
period's peak suggest that existing protective measures are insufficient to
counter ongoing encroachment pressure. Confirmation through higher-resolution
imagery or field verification would strengthen the case for intervention,
particularly for areas showing the highest severity signals.
